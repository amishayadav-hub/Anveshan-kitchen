AI Recipe Generator

/recipes/generate Live Updated Jul 2026

01What it is & why

A visitor types a dish they want to cook — or just the ingredients sitting in their kitchen — and gets four to five complete, cookable recipe variations back in a few seconds. Every variation swaps in Anveshan products where they naturally fit, and each one can be added to the cart in a click.

The recipes on this site exist to sell Anveshan products, not as an end in themselves. A fixed library only covers the dishes we've written up. The generator removes that ceiling: whatever someone wants to cook, we can show them how to cook it with Anveshan products and give them a direct path to buy. It turns “I don't have a recipe for that” into a sale.

02Goals & non-goals

Goals

  • Turn any dish or ingredient list into recipes that are actually correct and cookable.
  • Make Anveshan products the natural hero of every recipe, with a working add-to-cart.
  • Feel instant and reliable, in English and Hinglish.
  • Cost effectively nothing to run.

Non-goals

  • Not a nutrition or macro tracker.
  • No accounts or saved personal cookbooks.
  • Not a general chatbot — it only generates recipes.
  • Doesn't run checkout; the cart hands off to anveshan.farm.

03User flow

  1. Type a dish name and/or add ingredients as tags, and pick a language (English / Hinglish).
  2. Press Generate.
  3. Within a few seconds, see 4–5 variations of that one dish, each clearly different.
  4. Each variation shows ingredients (Anveshan ones highlighted), the method, prep/cook time and servings, a serving suggestion, and a “Shop the Anveshan products” footer with subtotal and Add to Cart.
  5. Regenerate for a fresh set, or add products and check out on anveshan.farm.

Behind the scenes: the request is first matched against a local library of ~10,000 Indian recipes (on-device, no API) to ground the dish in something real; the closest match guides generation but is never shown raw. A language model then produces the variations under a strict output contract, which are validated and cleaned before display. If the primary model is unavailable a second is tried; if both are down, English falls back to the closest library recipes rather than an error.

04Functional requirements

Input

  1. Accept a dish name (up to 200 characters) and/or up to 15 ingredient tags.
  2. Require at least one of dish or ingredients; otherwise show a clear inline message.
  3. Support English and Hinglish (Roman-script Hindi, never Devanagari). Switching language clears current results.
  4. Treat everything typed as data, not instructions — ignore embedded commands and never recommend a non-Anveshan brand.

Every generated recipe set

  1. Return 4–5 variations of a single dish.
  2. Each variation must be meaningfully different (regional style, signature add-in, technique, dietary swap, or format) and say how.
  3. Every ingredient must have a concrete quantity and standard unit — never “to taste”, never blank.
  4. Ratios and technique must be sound (flatbread dough isn't 1:1 flour:water; whole spices are tempered, not raw in a cold filling).
  5. Steps are ordered and cover the whole process; servings match the yield.
  6. Each variation uses at least one Anveshan product (aim 2–3) as a natural swap. Only genuine products are branded — a generic ingredient we don't sell stays generic.
  7. The products shown match the products actually used, and each maps to a correct, distinct purchasable item.

Commerce & presentation

  1. Each variation shows its Anveshan products with image, price and running subtotal, and an Add to Cart for all of them.
  2. The cart never double-counts — two ingredients resolving to the same product collapse into one line.
  3. Scroll the first result into view when generation finishes.
  4. Clear loading, empty and error states, each with a retry.
  5. Emit schema.org/Recipe structured data for the generated results.

05Quality bar

A generated set is good when:

  • 100% of ingredients have a real measurement; times and servings are present and plausible; nothing renders the literal word “null”.
  • No two variations are near-duplicates; each states its point of difference.
  • Every variation uses ≥1 correctly-branded, purchasable Anveshan product, and the cart subtotal is correct.
  • Hinglish output is Roman-script with no Devanagari.
  • Results return reliably in both languages under normal use.

06Edge cases & failure handling

  • No dish and no ingredients — blocked with an inline message.
  • Model rate-limited or slow — try the second model; English can fall back to the library; otherwise a friendly “try again in a moment”.
  • Malformed output — repaired and re-validated; a variation that can't be made complete is dropped, not shown broken.
  • Abuse — requests are rate-limited per visitor (15/minute).
  • A product we don't sell — never invented or mis-linked; left as a plain ingredient.

07Metrics & constraints

Success metrics

  • Generation success rate (a usable set returned).
  • Add-to-Cart rate from a generated recipe — this is a sales tool.
  • Median time to first result.
  • Regenerate rate (proxy for “first set wasn't good enough”).

Dependencies & constraints

  • Free API tiers + a local embedding model — ~zero cost at current volume.
  • Free tiers are rate-limited; heavy traffic needs paid keys.
  • Deployed on Vercel (serverless) — no self-hosted local model.
  • Product prices/links mirror the store and must stay in sync.

Recipes — Browse & Recipe Page

/recipes /recipes/[slug] Live Updated Jul 2026

01What it is & why

The curated recipe catalogue. /recipes lets a visitor browse and filter the full set; each recipe has its own page with the ingredients, method, tips, FAQ — and the Anveshan products it uses, ready to add to the cart.

Recipes are the hook; products are the sale. A good recipe page earns trust — “this is a real, well-written recipe” — and then makes it effortless to buy the exact ghee, oil, atta or superfood the recipe calls for. Browse exists to get people from “I want to cook something” to the right recipe page quickly.

02Goals & non-goals

Goals

  • Find a relevant recipe fast — by dish type, by Anveshan product, by veg/non-veg, or by name.
  • Make every recipe page genuinely useful to cook from.
  • Surface the products a recipe uses and make them one tap to add.
  • Load fast and be fully usable on a phone.

Non-goals

  • No accounts, ratings, comments, or user-submitted recipes here.
  • No on-site checkout — the cart hands off to anveshan.farm.
  • Not a nutrition tool.
  • Content is editorial/seeded, not user-generated.

03Browse flow

  1. A grid of recipe cards — image, name, short text, how many Anveshan products it uses and their combined price, and a veg/non-veg mark.
  2. A single scrollable row of category filters: “All Recipes” plus product groups (Ghee, Oil, Atta, Superfood) and dish types.
  3. Choosing a product group reveals a second row of sub-filters (Oil → Groundnut, Mustard, Sesame…).
  4. A Veg / Non-Veg toggle filters the grid; search by name filters live and syncs to the URL (?q=), so it's shareable and survives refresh.
  5. A result count is shown; no matches shows an empty state with “Reset filters”.
  6. The grid loads in batches and grows as you scroll; each card can add its Anveshan products to the cart without opening the recipe.

04Recipe page flow

  1. Header — image, title, an intro that expands, and Share.
  2. At-a-glance chips — total time (tap for prep vs cook), servings, difficulty, category.
  3. Serving scaler — 1×/2×/3× rescales every ingredient quantity on the page.
  4. Jump nav — sticky links to Ingredients / Method / Tips / Products / FAQ.
  5. Ingredients — a checkable list; Anveshan items are highlighted and offer a variety picker (ghee variety, atta variety) that drives the cart line.
  6. Method — numbered steps (long recipes show the first few with “See all steps”); product mentions link out.
  7. Tips & FAQ — tips collapse with “Load more”; an FAQ accordion when present.
  8. Shop the Anveshan products — each product with image, variant, price, subtotal, trust signals, and Add to Cart.
  9. Related recipes and a persistent sticky Add-to-Cart bar so buying is always one tap away.

05Functional requirements

Browse

  1. List all published recipes as cards showing image, name, short text, count + combined price of Anveshan products, and a veg/non-veg mark.
  2. Filter by top-level category and sub-product; filters combine with search and the veg/non-veg toggle.
  3. Name search syncs to ?q= (shareable, restorable); category reflects in ?category=.
  4. Show a live result count and a friendly empty state with a reset action.
  5. Render the grid in batches and extend on scroll; keep filtering responsive on a large list.
  6. Add a recipe's Anveshan products to the cart from the card.

Recipe page

  1. Show image, title, expandable intro, time (with prep/cook breakdown), servings, difficulty, category.
  2. Serving scaler (1×/2×/3×) rescales all ingredient quantities consistently.
  3. Checkable ingredients; Anveshan items branded, with a variety selector that drives the cart line.
  4. Ordered, numbered steps; product mentions in steps link to the product.
  5. Show tips and FAQ only when the recipe has them.
  6. Products panel with per-product variant/price/image, a correct subtotal, Add to Cart, plus a persistent sticky bar.
  7. Cart never double-counts; checkout redirects to anveshan.farm.
  8. Related recipes and a breadcrumb to the category.
  9. Emit schema.org/Recipe structured data and correct page metadata.

Both

  1. Fully responsive; legible on phones with no horizontal scrolling.
  2. Images a remote optimiser rejects load directly, so nothing appears broken.

06Edge cases & failure handling

  • No results — empty state + “Reset filters”.
  • Recipe with no Anveshan products — the products panel and Add-to-Cart are hidden; the recipe still reads fine.
  • Missing tips/FAQ — those sections are omitted, not shown empty.
  • Broken/blocked image — falls back so the layout never shows a broken image.
  • Bad ?category= / ?q= — ignored gracefully (defaults to all).

07Metrics & constraints

Success metrics

  • Add-to-Cart rate from recipe pages and browse cards — the primary measure.
  • Browse → recipe-page click-through.
  • Search usage and search→result rate.
  • Core Web Vitals, especially on mobile.

Dependencies & constraints

  • Database for recipe/product content; product data must match the live store.
  • Cart hands off to anveshan.farm for payment.
  • Recipe pages are statically generated for speed — avoid anything that forces per-request rendering on them.
